- Identification under recursive ordering; could identify using other methods and change bkConditional


- Joint Scenarios :
	- Stagflation: oil up and unemployment up together. Singly, each is a demand-type shock in this model; jointly they force the system onto a supply-shock path, which is a genuinely different exercise.
	- US recession with no policy response: constrain the US IP path and hold the rate fixed. That's a counterfactual policy experiment — what the economy does if the Bank doesn't cut — and it's the kind of question the WZ setup is actually built for.
	- Tariff shock: constrain US activity down and the exchange rate up simultaneously, since a tariff hits both and constraining only one lets the model offset it through the other.

- Partial-horizon conditions already work via .window and are underused. "Oil spikes for six months then normalises" is more realistic than a permanent plateau, and it lets the model tell you the persistence rather than you imposing it.

- In-sample counterfactuals. Nothing restricts WZ to the forecast horizon. Re-run it over 2022–23 holding the rate at 0.25% and you get "what if the Bank hadn't tightened" — same algebra, different sample. That's a paper-sized exercise on its own.

- Soft conditions. WZ's original contribution includes conditioning on a distribution for the constrained variable rather than a point path — oil in [110, 130] rather than exactly 120. That gives the constrained variable a band too, which is more honest for something labelled a risk scenario, and it's a modest change to the same projection algebra.
